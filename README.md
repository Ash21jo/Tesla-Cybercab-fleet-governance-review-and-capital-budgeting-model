##Tesla Cybercab fleet: governance review and capital budgeting model

Should Tesla invest US$2.55 billion in a fleet of 60,000 Cybercab robotaxis? This project answers that question with a corporate governance review of Tesla and a full capital budgeting model in Excel. The recommendation is to reject the programme under current assumptions.

Individual project for Advanced Corporate Finance (FI502E), MSc International Finance, Rennes School of Business, 2026.

The project being valued
60,000 Cybercabs deployed in three waves: 5,000 in 2027, 20,000 in 2028 and 35,000 in 2029
Ten-year horizon (2027 to 2036), no terminal value
Base fare of US$11.50 per ride, 15 rides per vehicle per day, 4.5 miles per trip, 35% empty miles
Operating cost of US$0.45 per mile and fixed cost of US$12,000 per vehicle per year
US$32,000 per vehicle plus US$4,000 for depot and charging
Part 1: Corporate governance

Based on Tesla's FY2025 Form 10-K/A and its governance documents.

Six of the nine directors are independent, and the chair is not the CEO.
The CEO's pay award is tied to market capitalisation and operational milestones such as robotaxis in operation, not to returns on capital.
Tesla does business with other companies the CEO controls, including a US$2 billion investment in SpaceX and US$626 million of Megapack sales to SpaceX in the first nine months of 2026.
Part 2: Valuation
Item	Result
Cost of equity	18.40%
WACC	18.29%
NPV (WACC, APV and FCFE methods)	-US$504 million
IRR	11.03%
Total capex	US$2,554 million
Peak funding need	US$2,498 million (5.5% of Tesla's cash)

All three valuation methods give the same NPV. Tesla's debt weight is under 1%, so the interest tax shield is worth only US$0.3 million and financing barely changes the value.

Risk analysis
Scenarios: five probability-weighted cases (base case, Tesla target economics, regulatory delay, safety incident, tariffs). The probability-weighted NPV is -US$794 million, and 85% of the probability sits on scenarios with a negative NPV.
Monte Carlo: 483 draws. 66% end in a loss, and the mean NPV is -US$249 million.
Sensitivity: fare per ride and utilisation move the NPV the most.
Break-even: US$12.42 per ride (8% above base) or 16.9 rides per vehicle per day (13% above base).
Delay test: waiting one to four years does not turn the NPV positive.
Recommendation

Reject the 60,000-vehicle programme under current assumptions. Bring it back for a new vote only if real fares are above US$12.42 per ride or utilisation is above 16.9 rides per day, with unit costs at or below plan.

Limitations

Most operating inputs are my own estimates from secondary sources, because no company has yet run a driverless fleet of this size. The cost of capital uses Tesla's own beta and capital structure, which may not reflect the risk of a ride-hail business. The model includes no terminal value and no option to renew the fleet after eight years.
